IMARA.

The Integrated Meta-Analysis Resource for Africa brings together what the published literature says about lymphoma, leukaemia and myeloma in African patients: who is affected, how they are treated, and how they fare.

Population-registry studies (59 papers, 214,657 patient records) are counted separately so they do not swamp the clinical series.

11,137 patients from 92 studies, rebuilt from published survival curves and pooled. A mix of clinical trials and observational cohorts across all disease groups; each patient counted once.

HIV

People living with HIV face a higher risk of death, mostly in the first year

HIV-positive (730) HIV-negative (964)
Hazard ratio, HIV+ vs HIV−1.40

95% confidence interval 1.19 to 1.65, from 15 studies and 1,694 patients. Each study's HIV-positive patients are compared only with its own HIV-negative patients, so differences between studies do not drive the result.

The excess is in the first year: hazard ratio 1.56 (1.28–1.91) in months 0–12 and 1.14 (0.86–1.51) after.

Where

Published data come from 43 African countries

Large parts of the continent have little or no published evidence. Showing where the gaps are is as much a part of the resource as the numbers themselves.

How it was built

A living review of the published literature

Search

Systematic searches of the international and African medical literature, in several languages.

Screen

Every candidate study checked for African patients with a blood cancer and data of their own.

Extract

Patient numbers, characteristics, treatment and outcomes recorded study by study, each value traceable to its source.

Check and pool

Values cross-checked, the same patients counted once, and results pooled with standard meta-analysis methods.
